This feature is a gym logger with the depth of a dedicated app. It includes an exercise library, routines and multi-week programs with progression, set-by-set logging, a rest timer, PRs and charts, Apple Health, Live Activity, Siri and Apple Watch logging. Cloning Strong or Hevy is not the goal. The goal is to be good enough at the gym that the integration (the schedule, the food log, mood, rewards, Claude over MCP) is reason enough to keep it all in one app.

## What the repo already decides

1. **A logged set is a statement, not a reading.** `health-data.md` forbids keeping a copy of Health *readings* ("There is no table, and there must not be one"), but entries the person writes (food log, mood, doses) are stored. Sets typed in the app are entries, so they get tables. Workouts *other apps* wrote to Health (a run) are readings: queried on demand, never stored.
2. **Nothing derives anything from a body weight.** The weight rule rules out BMI, "trending" and any `HealthRuleMetric` on weight. So there are no bodyweight-relative standards ("1.5x bodyweight bench"), no Wilks/DOTS, and no automatic bodyweight term in pull-up volume. A dedicated app has these; this one deliberately won't.
3. **Absent stays absent.** A session with no measured energy writes no energy to Health. Calories are never estimated from sets.
4. **Writes to Health happen on a person's tap and are retractable.** That is the food-log pattern: its own switch, sample ids kept (`healthSampleIds`), and `deleteHealthSamples` on delete or edit.
5. **The app proposes; the person confirms.** This applies to `energyBudget.ts` and the AI estimates, and it applies to progression: the next weight is a suggestion, never written into a plan unasked.
6. **Derived, never stored.** The coin balance is a ledger sum. PRs, estimated 1RMs and "which routine is next" in a program are all computed from session history, never kept as their own fields.
7. **Only a person moves coins.** Finishing a session completes its linked task (a person's action), and that earns coins. Sets earn nothing ("Subtasks earn nothing, or splitting a task would be the way to earn more"). PRs earn nothing.
8. **A step that runs out waits.** This comes from `focus-sessions.md`: a finished rest timer chimes and counts up, and never advances the workout by itself.
9. **Adding a Health read type costs existing installs a re-ask.** There is no re-prompt, so the person has to tap ask again in Health settings.
10. **Every tap-driven sheet that holds a drag is `fullScreen`.** Workout mode reorders exercises mid-session, so it is `fullScreen` like `EditorSheet` and `CookModeSheet`.

## Options considered

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Workouts as tasks only (each exercise a subtask) | Rejected. Subtasks have no sets, weights or history per exercise, and they'd bloat the task table by about 25 rows per session. |
| **Separate workout records, linked to a scheduled task** | **Chosen.** The task owns *when* (recurrence, a 3-per-week quota, vacation, streaks, coins). The session owns *what happened*. |
| Health as the store for sets | Rejected. HealthKit has no per-set weight and reps model. |
| Exercise library as synced rows | Rejected. Hundreds of rows on every device, and updating the library would mean a migration. |
| **Library in code, custom exercises and overrides as rows** | **Chosen.** Stable ids (`lib:barbell-bench-press`), and it updates with the app. |
| Routine items as a child table | Rejected for v1. A routine is edited as a unit (the `saved_meals.items` precedent), so a JSON column is enough. |
| **Sets as a child table** | **Chosen.** Sets are appended one at a time from the phone and the watch, so they need row-level sync and dedupe by id. |

## Decided for this feature

- **Sessions and sets are part of the health record.** `workout_sessions` and `workout_sets` join `HEALTH_SYNC_TABLES`, so iCloud never receives them, and a sync server receives them only once the person turns on `syncServerHealthLogs`. The reason is the one written on that list: App Review guideline 5.1.3(ii) says an app "may not store personal health information in iCloud", and this app takes the strict reading for logs the person typed in. A workout log is a record of what a body did. Once phase 9 attaches heart rate and energy from HealthKit, it is plainly covered. Routines, programs and custom exercises stay ordinary synced data, because they are plans rather than records (the same reason `saved_meals` isn't withheld). The cost is that workout history doesn't cross devices for someone with no sync server.
- **Rest timers respect quiet hours.** This is the opposite of recipe step timers, which ring through. Inside quiet hours the alarm is not scheduled, and the in-app haptic still fires when the timer ends with the app in front. That is how the task timer's `scheduleTimerAlarm` already behaves.
- **The watch goes all the way to a HealthKit workout session** (phases 8 and 9). Phase 9 started with a written review against `health-data.md` before any code ("Heart rate during a workout" there).
- **Heart rate is shown, never interpreted.** Live on the watch, and the average, maximum and time in zones on the phone's workout page, read back from Health and never stored. Never judged, never compared to a norm, never a `HealthRuleMetric`, never a generated task. The zones' maximum is typed, or 220 minus age, always labeled as the estimate it is.

## The design

### Data model

The new types go in `src/types/index.ts`, and the tables go in `initDatabase` with indexes in the migrations array.

- **`exercises`** (custom exercises and overrides of built-in ones): `id`, `name`, `kind` (`weight_reps | bodyweight_reps | assisted_reps | duration | distance | weight_distance`), `primaryMuscles` and `secondaryMuscles` (JSON), `equipment`, `barKg` (nullable), `notes`, `otherNames` (JSON, other names the person knows it by), `archived`, `isCustom`. An override row reuses the built-in's id, so renaming "Bench press" to "Flat bench" is one row.
- **`workout_routines`**: `id`, `name`, `notes`, `programId` (nullable), `sortOrder`, and `items` (JSON). Each item holds `{ exerciseId, supersetKey?, restSeconds, progression, targets: [{ kind: warmup|working|drop|amrap, repsMin, repsMax, weightKg?, pctOfTm?, rpe? }] }`.
- **`workout_programs`**: `id`, `name`, `weeks`, `rotation` (an ordered list of routine ids), `scheme` (`double | linear | percent | rpe`, or none: null in the type, the word `none` in the NOT NULL column, which an older build reads as `double`), `trainingMaxes` (JSON, per exercise), `startedAt`, `archived`. The program's current week and next routine are derived from its sessions.
- **`workout_sessions`**: `id`, `routineId?`, `programId?`, `taskId?` (the occurrence it completed), `title`, `startedAt`, `endedAt` (null while live), `notes`, `effort` (1 to 10, typed), `healthWorkoutId?`, `healthWritePending`, `measuredOnWatch` (phase 9: the Apple Watch measured it and saves it to Health itself).
- **`workout_sets`**: `id`, `sessionId`, `exerciseId`, `position`, `setIndex`, `kind`, `weightKg?`, `reps?`, `durationSeconds?`, `distanceM?`, `rpe?`, `completedAt?`, `supersetKey?`, `note?`. Indexes are `(session_id)` and `(exercise_id, completed_at)`. Weight is always stored in kg, and the unit is display only, like `waterUnit`.
- **Task link**: `Task.workoutRoutineId` (built in phase 2), like `logSavedMealId`, or `Task.workoutProgramId` (built in phase 4), which starts the program's next routine; a task names one or the other, never both. A template does not seed it: like a saved meal, a routine is a row of this person's own, which a template shared with somebody else (or run before the routine synced) would name and not find (`templateItemParity.test.ts`, "names a workout routine outside the template"). The MCP writes and reads it (`create_task`/`update_task`, `get_task`'s `workout`).
- **Settings** (the `settings` table, added to `SYNCED_SETTING_KEYS`): `workoutUnit`, `plateInventory` (bar weights, plates and counts), `defaultRestSeconds`. `workoutHealthWrite` (phase 5) is device-local instead, like `healthWriteEnabled`: it says what this phone may write to Health.

**Registration.** Each table also needs:
- an entry in `SYNC_TRACKED_TABLES` and in `BACKUP_TABLES` (the schema-completeness tests in `database.test.ts` fail until it has both);
- a `NATURAL_KEYS` rule for custom exercise names, so two devices that both create "Cable fly" offline fold into one, with `REFERENCES` from `workout_sets.exercise_id` and the routine items;
- a place on the `useTaskStore.initialize` reload list, which is how sync, demo mode and restore reach a store. No test checks this.

**Privacy class.** `workout_sessions` and `workout_sets` go in `HEALTH_SYNC_TABLES` (see "Decided for this feature"). Nothing tests membership of that list, so phase 1 adds a test that pins it. The sync settings copy ("Whether the mood, medication and food logs go to the sync server") names workouts too.

### Logic modules (pure, tested 1:1)

New files in `src/utils/`, each with its test in `src/__tests__/`. The first three are built; the rest are planned.

- `src/utils/exerciseLibrary.ts` (built): about 250 built-in exercises (name, kind, muscles, equipment, a short text cue), with ids written out rather than derived, since an id is forever. No video.
- `src/utils/exerciseImages.ts` (built): start and end photos for most of the library, from free-exercise-db (public domain, Unlicense). Not bundled: fetched from the dataset's own hosting when an exercise's page opens, only while Settings › Workouts › Exercise photos is on, and that switch ships off. The slug-to-photo map is written and checked by hand, because matching by name paired the wrong movements (a barbell bench press with the dumbbell one); an exercise with no confident match has no photos, and so does every custom one. A failed load hides the pair. Workout mode shows them behind a "Show form photos" row, collapsed by default because its sets are the point of that screen and photos above them would push them down; the open state lasts for the session.
- **Equipment is a closed set with specific machines in it** (`EXERCISE_EQUIPMENT`, `workoutData.ts`): leg press, leg curl, chest press, treadmill and the like each have a value, so a gym can say which machines it has. `machine` and `cardio_machine` stay as the catch-alls for a machine with no value of its own, and `machineParent` says which catch-all a specific one sits under. A gym that ticked a catch-all before the specific machines existed is read as having all of them (`equipmentAvailable` in `gyms.ts`), so no saved gym lost anything and nothing was migrated. A new machine is one value in the type, `STRENGTH_MACHINES` or `CARDIO_MACHINES`, a label, and the library rows filed under it. Lat pulldowns and rows on a stack stay `cable`.
- `src/utils/exerciseCatalog.ts` (built): merges the library with the custom rows, plus search (the substring scoring task search uses, without its scattered-letters fallback, which matched half the library) and muscle and equipment filters. An override row contributes only the name, other names, notes, bar weight and archived flag; what the movement is stays the library's. **Other names** are the person's own words for an exercise ("Two arm dumbbell row" for "Barbell row"). Search (the picker, the Exercises list, `list_exercises`) and the CSV import's name match read them and nothing else does; a result found only by one says "Also called …" under it. They are cleaned like a name (`cleanOtherNames`), may not repeat another exercise on offer's name or other name (`otherNameTaken`, so a name never answers for two exercises), and an import match on one is decided for the person like an exact name, since they wrote it. Two copies of one exercise folded by sync keep both lists. The import still remembers nothing: confirming a suggestion there does not add an other name.
- `src/utils/workoutData.ts` (built): the closed vocabularies, their labels, the resolve-or-shrug readers every row mapper uses, and the rest and plate settings' defaults and parsers.
- `src/utils/workoutProgression.ts` (built, phase 4): given the history for an exercise and its rule, propose the next session's targets:
  - **double:** hit the top of the rep range on every working set, then add an increment;
  - **linear:** add an increment after a success, and after N failures propose a 10% deload;
  - **percent:** sets taken from a training max, and the training max is bumped at the end of a cycle;
  - **rpe:** adjust the load from the last top set's RPE.

  It returns a proposal plus its reason ("Up 2.5 kg because every set last time reached 8."), and it rounds to loads you can make with your plates.
- `src/utils/workoutProgram.ts` (built, phase 4): where a program is, derived from its finished sessions (`programProgress`), and its words.
- `src/utils/workoutProgramLibrary.ts` (built, phase 4): the four built-in programs (`PROGRAM_LIBRARY`).
- `src/utils/plateMath.ts` (built): load, bar and plate inventory give the plates per side. It also builds warm-up ramps.
- `src/utils/workoutRecords.ts` (built): PR detection and estimated 1RM (Epley, only from sets of 10 reps or fewer, always labelled "estimated"). Best set, rep PRs at each rep count, and volume per session, exercise and muscle per week. Memoized per set array, the way `openTasksOf` is cached.
- `src/utils/workoutSession.ts` (built): the live session's rules, shaped like `cookMode.ts` and `focusPlan.ts`: the walking order with supersets alternated round by round (`walkOrder`, `nextSet`), where a logged set leaves the screen and whether it rests (`afterLog`), a routine's planned sets (`plannedSetsFor`), last time's sets and their match to this time's (`previousSets`, `matchPrevious`), "Add set" and "Same as last set" (`copyLastSet`, `valuesFromLastDone`), what finishing drops (`finishPlan`) and the summary (`sessionSummary`).
- `src/utils/workoutRoutine.ts` (built): the routine editor's edits, one set at a time (`withSetKind`, `withSetWeight`, `withSetReps`, `duplicateSet`, `removeSet`, `addSet`, `addWarmup`, with `withWarmupMode` and `withProgression` for the item), supersets as runs of neighbours (`supersetRoles`, `joinPrevious`, `leaveSuperset`, `normalizeSupersets`, and `droppedIntoSuperset`/`settleOpenSlots` for the editor's empty superset, a staged slot that never reaches Save) and the summary lines.
- `src/utils/workoutTask.ts` (built): what finishing a workout does to the task it was started from (`workoutTaskEffect`).
- `src/utils/workoutCsv.ts` (built, phase 7): reads Strong and Hevy CSV exports, matches their exercise names to the catalog, plans an idempotent import and writes the person's own log as a CSV. See "Phase 7, as built".

### Store

`src/store/useWorkoutStore.ts` (built in phase 1; phase 2 added laying a routine out, `addSessionExercise`, `addPlannedSet`, `lastTimeFor`, `addFinishedSession`, `replaceSets` and the task link):
- it holds exercises, routines, programs and sessions, with sets loaded per session or exercise on demand;
- its actions are `startSession`, `logSet`, `editSet`, `deleteSet`, `reorderExercises`, `finishSession`, `discardSession`, plus CRUD for routines, programs and custom exercises;
- `finishSession` answers the linked task through the task store, never the reverse: `completeTask`, or `logQuotaUnit` for a target below its count (`workoutTaskEffect`). Ticking the task off without a session does not invent a session.
- a workout done earlier is logged from `LogPastWorkoutSheet` (Workouts › … › Log a past workout) through `buildPastWorkout` (`src/utils/pastWorkout.ts`) into `addFinishedSession`: staged until Save, a blank set dropped and a non-number refused by name, never ending in the future, and its Health write left to the pending pass (`healthWritePending`). Mock: `workouts-mocks/past-workout.png`.
- a custom exercise is never removed, only archived, because sets logged on another device never arrive over iCloud and can't be counted from here; an archived custom exercise keeps its name (the database's unique `name_key`); deleting a built-in's row puts the library's back. Deleting a session deletes its sets in the same transaction.

### Rest timer

- **Mechanism.** Generalise the step timers rather than building a fourth timer. They are the only timers that run to the second, have +1 min and restart, ring through quiet hours via AlarmKit and survive backgrounding. `StepTimer` is now a subject union (built): `RecipeStepTimer` (`kind: 'recipe'`, `recipeId`, `stepId`) and `RestTimer` (`kind: 'rest'`, `sessionId`, `exerciseId`). The store, alarm and Live Activity plumbing are reused.
- **Behaviour.** Logging a set starts the rest for that exercise automatically. When the rest ends it chimes and counts up, and the cursor stays put.
- **A rest can be set mid-workout, per exercise.** The Rest chip on the exercise card writes `WorkoutSession.restOverrides` (exercise id to seconds, 0 for none; JSON in `workout_sessions.rest_overrides`) through `setSessionRest`. `restSecondsFor` reads it before the routine item's rest and the default, and `tickSet`, `watchSetRows` and the watch's per-exercise `rest` all pass it, so the phone and the watch agree. It never edits the routine: the finish summary offers a `rest` change (`routineChanges`, only for an exercise the workout did, and only when it differs from the item's rest or the default), which the person ticks like any other. The chip is hidden on a superset's earlier exercises, since the rest comes after the round. A running rest keeps its length; the new one applies from the next set.
- **Quiet hours.** The subject decides whether quiet hours apply: `rest` respects them and `recipe` keeps ringing through. One store with two policies, not two stores.

### UI (each piece mocked before it is built)

**Screens:**
- **Workouts**: history list, calendar dots on the month grid, and a "Start" for the next routine in the active program.
- **Exercise detail** (built as a sheet in phase 3, see below): estimated 1RM, best-set and volume line chart (the `WeightChart` shape), PR table and history.
- **Routine editor**: an `EditorSheet`-based list of exercises with drag reorder, per-set targets and a progression picker.
- **Program editor and library**: generic built-ins (5x5 linear, Push/Pull/Legs, Upper/Lower, a 4-week percentage wave). They avoid trademarked program names.

**Workout mode** is a `fullScreen` sheet modelled on `CookModeSheet`:
- the screen stays awake;
- one exercise card at a time, with sets as rows whose weight and reps are typed in place under the table's own headings (`parseSetCell`, written on blur), and a plate line ("20 + 10 + 2.5 per side");
- you tap a set's check to log it, and the rest timer starts in the footer;
- a "finish" summary shows PRs hit, and its Save also writes the workout to Health while that is switched on (phase 5).

**Stats** gains a WORKOUTS section: sessions per week, sets per muscle per week and recent PRs.

**Mood** gains one more `ContrastBars` pair: mood on training days vs rest days, under the mood-log rules (at least 3 days on each side, association not cause). It counts only the app's own sessions, because a day with no Health workout is ambiguous.

### Health (native)

- **Write.** A finished session becomes an `HKWorkout` (`.traditionalStrengthTraining`). Energy is attached only if a watch workout session measured it. The UUID is kept in `healthWorkoutId`, and deleting or editing the session retracts and rewrites it, like `reviseEntry`. This adds a `'workout'` write kind with its own switch, extends the combined authorization request (share and read together, per the revocation note in the bridge), and updates the plist strings.
- **Read.** Workouts from other apps appear in the Workouts history as "From Health", queried per window and never stored. This is a new read type, so existing installs have to re-ask.
- `activeEnergyBoost` stays as it is. A watch session's energy reaches it through Health, so nothing is counted twice.

### Live Activity and Siri (native)

- **Live Activity.** A new `Workout` kind shows the current exercise, "set 3 of 4" and the rest countdown. Its buttons are deep links (`dundundun://workout?do=set-done|rest-add|skip-rest`), the way Focus does it. Every other kind has an empty `ContentState` and is restarted on each change; this one is the first to update its `ContentState` in place (built in phase 6, see "Phase 6, as built").
- **Siri.** "Start my workout" and "Start Push A" use a routine `AppEntity` resolved against an App Group index (the `pantryIndex.ts` pattern). Logging sets by voice is out of scope.

### Apple Watch (native, the largest piece)

**Phase W1, logging without HealthKit:**
- a Workout page in the existing watch app with the current exercise, the target set, and weight and reps on the Digital Crown with Done Set;
- the live session reaches the watch in the existing `WATCH_CAPS` snapshot;
- sets come back as a new tap kind `set` on the quiet-tap queue, deduped by a set id generated on the watch;
- a watch-first session (phone in the locker) needs the routine on the watch, and that is the hard part.

**Phase W2, `HKWorkoutSession` on the watch (built in phase 9):** heart rate, measured energy, the app staying in front on the wrist, and the workout ring. It needed the HealthKit entitlement on the watch target, new share and read types, and its own review under `health-data.md` ("Heart rate during a workout"), which keeps HRV ruled out (resting heart rate was let in later for a display-only list on the Blood pressure screen).

### MCP

Each phase ships its own slice of the MCP server:
- **reads:** `list_exercises`, `list_workouts` (sessions with sets), `exercise_history` (PRs, estimated 1RM trend, last session);
- **writes:** `save_routine`, `save_program`, `log_workout` (after the fact), `update_workout`, `delete_workout`, each with a `withAgentLedger` wrapper and `COUNTERPARTS` entries (built through phase 4, with `next_workout` for the proposals);
- **text:** the rules in `instructions.ts` (kg on the wire; a progression is a proposal; never fabricate a session);
- **adoption:** `ADOPTION_CHECKS` entries.

Because sessions and sets are health-record tables, the MCP server sees workout history only on a phone that sends health logs to the sync server. Routines and programs reach it either way, so Claude can build a plan for someone who hasn't shared their history. `get_overview`'s `healthLogs` counts gain a `workouts` entry.

This is the strongest differentiator: "build me a 4-day upper/lower around my Tuesday commitments" becomes four `save_routine` calls, a `save_program` and recurring tasks, all in one replica.

### Gating and navigation

- A new **Workouts** switch in Feature areas, beside kitchen and rewards. It is off by default, since a big area nobody asked for is clutter.
- Simple mode hides the area through a `SimpleFeatureId` with `contentScreen`.
- Workouts has a menu row of its own, after Groceries & meals, dropped while the switch is off (`NavDestination.workouts`). It started as a member of the Health hub so the menu stayed at fifteen rows, and moved out once it held routines, programs and an exercise library: a place you go to train sits with the other working surfaces, not with the logs you look back at. Its sub-screens are sheets and stack routes. The screen can be picked as a tab through `tabRoutes`.
- Its settings are a group of their own, Settings › Workouts (`workoutsOnly`, next to Groceries & meals), rather than a section of Apple Health. Apple Health is `iosOnly`, which left the unit and the rest timer unreachable anywhere else. The switch that writes workouts to Health stays in Apple Health › Log to Health with the rest of what the app writes there.

### What it deliberately does not do

- Bodyweight-relative standards, Wilks/DOTS, or a body-fat estimate (the weight rule).
- Calories estimated from lifting.
- Social features, coaches or shared feeds (there is no backend). A routine can still be shared as a file through the share sheet.
- Exercise videos. Photos are fetched, never bundled (see `exerciseImages.ts`); no free video set was found with a license that allows shipping it.
- Auto-advancing a session, or auto-applying a progression.

## Phase 2, as built

What phase 2 settled that the plan above left open. Files: `RoutineEditorSheet.tsx`, `WorkoutModeSheet.tsx`, `WorkoutModeHost.tsx` and `ExercisePickerSheet.tsx` in `src/components/`, `useStartWorkout.ts` and `useRestTimer.ts` in `src/hooks/`, `useWorkoutModeStore.ts`, and the three utils above. The routine editor and workout mode follow the approved mocks; the Workouts screen's routine list, the exercise picker, the finish summary and the task row's button were mocked in the PR.

- **Planned sets are rows with no `completedAt`.** Starting from a routine lays its sets out as rows (`startSession`), so they can be shown, edited and checked one at a time; finishing deletes the ones never checked, because a plan nobody carried out records nothing. A set's numbers come from the routine's target weight, else from last time's matching set; reps from last time, else the top of the range. Neither is a progression: nothing here moves a load on.
- **PREVIOUS matches by kind**: the nth warm-up against last time's nth warm-up, the nth working set against the nth working set, so adding a warm-up doesn't shift every row onto the wrong one.
- **A logged set is the only thing that moves the screen.** On its own, an exercise keeps its card and the set starts the rest. In a superset the card turns to the partner with no rest, and the set that closes the round starts it (the mock's "rest after the pair"). A finished exercise keeps its card; moving on is a tap.
- **A superset can be made or ended mid-workout from the exercise card's "•••" menu** (`joinSessionSuperset`/`leaveSessionSuperset`, over `joinedSuperset`/`leftSuperset` in `workoutSession.ts`). It sits in a menu rather than a pill because it is rare and the card is already busy. Joining moves the partner (and its own superset) to sit right below, so a superset stays a run of neighbours, and the key goes on done and planned sets alike. Leaving follows the routine editor's rule: out of the middle splits the run, a partner left alone leaves too. The watch does it from the bottom of an exercise's set list ("Superset with…", "Leave superset"), by a `superset` tap (`itemId` the exercise, `exerciseId` the partner, absent to leave) that the phone applies through the same store actions; the watch draws the same change over its list meanwhile (`WatchWorkoutEdit.superset`, which mirrors `joinedSuperset`/`leftSuperset` and lays the sets back out round by round).
- **A rest is one timer at a time**, a `RestTimer` in `useStepTimerStore` beside the recipe step timers (the subject union). Logging the next set replaces it. It is a plain notification that quiet hours suppress, never an AlarmKit alarm: a full-screen alarm to dismiss after every set is the wrong weight for a rest. `useRestChime`, mounted once in `WorkoutModeHost`, buzzes when it ends with the app in front, whatever screen is showing. It never moves the workout on.
- **The rest's Live Activity rides the step timer's** from JS alone: key `rest:<id>`, kind `rest`, the session title over "Rest after set 2 of …". Its Done is the existing `stopTimer` link, which for a `rest:` key skips the rest. No Swift changed; phase 6 builds the workout's own activity.
- **A rest outlives nothing.** `WorkoutModeHost` drops every rest whose session is no longer live (`removeRestsOutside`), however it ended (finished, discarded, deleted on another device), so the workout store never reaches into the timers and stays loadable by the MCP server.
- **Finishing answers the task the way its checkbox would** (`workoutTaskEffect`): complete it, log one unit on a target below its count ("3 a week"), ask its question first (the host queues it once workout mode closes, the answer-first rule), or leave it alone (already done, not due) and say so on the summary. A rotation answers with `pick` when the session's routine or program is one a member names (`RotationItem.workoutRoutineId`/`workoutProgramId`, found by `rotationMemberForWorkout`, which prefers a member still short of its count) and logs that member through `logRotationUnit`; a rotation with no such member, or one that asks a question on completion, is left alone. The link is read off the session rather than stored on it, and the member's pill starts the workout in place of logging a pick, while the checkbox's picker still logs one by hand. Three rules ride on it:
  - **Members share one task id**, so anything asking "is this the workout I asked for?" from a task id alone gets the wrong answer: `startWorkout`'s `perWorkout` makes a live session match the routine or program as well, and a new such check does the same.
  - **A template never carries the link** (`normalizeTemplateItem` drops it), for the task-level link's reason in `templateItemParity.test.ts`: it names a row of this person's own. So the template editor has no Workout line.
  - **The widget, Lock Screen and watch paths need nothing of their own**: they end in `finishSession` or `addWatchSession`, which hold the rule (the watch path logs only when the workout ended today, like a target's unit).
- **Leaving is not ending.** End asks: Close keeps the session live (the Workouts screen shows it as In progress with Resume), Discard deletes it. One session at a time: starting another while one is live offers to open it.
- **Weights step in the shown unit** by 2.5 kg or 5 lb, through `CountStepper`'s new `places` (decimal keyboard, a typed 82.75 kept to the quarter), converted at the stepper (`weightToDisplay`, `weightFromDisplay`, kept to three places of a kilogram so 135 lb reads back as 135).
- **The progression is a stored choice** on each routine item; phase 4 turned it into a suggestion in workout mode. Warm-ups were a count in phase 2; phase 4 added the ramp.
- **The finish screen** shows time, exercises, sets and each exercise's heaviest set, takes effort (1 to 10, optional) and notes, and says what saving drops and what it does to the task. The notes are on the exercise's card, not on its sets.
- **Settings.** The Workouts switch is in Feature areas beside kitchen and rewards. The unit and the default rest are a Workouts section under Apple Health, shown while the area is on.
- **Not built in phase 2:** a floating drag of the exercise cards themselves (workout mode reorders from a compact list behind its Reorder pill, one row per `exerciseBlocks` block so a superset moves whole, or a place at a time from the "•••" menu through `moveSessionExercise`, the way the watch does; the cards are too tall to drag and the page scroll can't autoscroll under them), a floating bar for a live session on every screen (the Workouts screen's card is the way back), and the plate line and suggestion card in the mock (phases 3 and 4).

## Phase 3, as built

What phase 3 settled that the plan above left open. Files: `src/utils/workoutRecords.ts`, `workoutRecordText.ts` (the words a record is shown as), `plateMath.ts` and `exerciseChart.ts` (where the chart's points and breaks fall) in `src/utils/`; `ExerciseDetailSheet.tsx`, `ExerciseProgressChart.tsx`, `PlateLine.tsx` and `WorkoutStatsCards.tsx` in `src/components/`; `useWorkoutStats.ts` in `src/hooks/`; `trainingDayMoodContrast` in `moodInsights.ts`; `exercise_history` in `mcp/src/workoutTools.ts`. `src/__bench__/workoutRecords.bench.ts` times it over a year of sets (about 30 ms cold in Node for every exercise, nothing warm). The exercise page, the Stats section and the mood row were mocked before they were built (the exercise page is `workouts-mocks/exercise-detail.png`).

**The records, and what makes one:**
- **Nothing is stored.** `analyzeExercise` walks one exercise's sets oldest to newest and returns its chart points, its standing bests and every record any session broke. `analyzeAll` does it for every exercise at once (Stats). Both are memoized per array of sets, the way `openTasksOf` is per task array, and the key is the array's identity: the store never mutates one.
- **A set counts when it was done and isn't a warm-up** (`countsForRecords`). Drop sets and AMRAP sets (shown as "To failure") are working sets. A planned set has no `completedAt` and counts for nothing.
- **A session is judged as a whole against everything before it.** Three improving sets are one record at the best figure, not three events. A record is announced only where there is something earlier to beat, so a first session, or a rep count nobody had done, sets no record.
- **The estimated 1RM is Epley from a set of 10 reps or fewer** (`E1RM_MAX_REPS`), rounded to 0.1 kg, and a single is the weight itself. Every label that shows it says "estimated"; the MCP field is `estimatedKg` with a note.
- **"Best set at N reps" is the heaviest set of N or more reps.** A heavier set of ten retires the best of eight, since it dominates it. A rep count is listed, and announced, only while the set holding it has exactly N reps (up to `REP_RECORD_MAX`, 12), or one set would read as eight records.
- **Other kinds have the same two readings in their own unit.** A bodyweight or assisted exercise ranks by reps and its volume is its reps, **never reps times a body weight**; an assisted set's weight is the help, so it is never multiplied either. A hold ranks by its longest set and sums seconds, a distance by its farthest and sums metres, a carry by its heaviest load and sums metres. Only weight and reps has an estimated 1RM (`metricsFor`).
- **Muscles credit a primary muscle 1 set and a secondary 0.5** (`SECONDARY_MUSCLE_WEIGHT`), the common fractional-set convention, once per set (a muscle listed as both counts as primary). Weekly volume in kilograms is summed only for weight and reps, since volumes in other units don't add. Weeks start on the person's `weekStartsOn`, and a session's day is its logical day (`dayResetTime`), never a cut of `toISOString()`.
- **Stats counts finished workouts only** (`useWorkoutStats`): a session in progress can still have its sets unchecked, so none of the bars, muscle sets or records read it.

**The exercise page is a sheet, not a route.** It opens from the Exercises list and from the exercise's name in workout mode, and workout mode is a full-screen modal, where a pushed route would sit behind it. A sheet nests inside that modal the way the exercise picker does and closing it leaves the workout where it was. The chart's x axis is the calendar (a layoff is a gap, not a squeeze), the line breaks after `CHART_GAP_DAYS` (21) with no session, the y axis is never anchored at zero (`chartDomain`, for `WeightChart`'s reason), and only the latest record's dot is `colors.done`. The sheet is `fullScreen` with a `useSafeAreaInsets().top` inset and a Done button, because the chart is scrubbed by a drag and a page sheet's dismissal pan cancels it (the rule above); it still nests inside workout mode's modal. "New" marks a record row that a session in the last `NEW_RECORD_DAYS` (14) days actually broke (`isNewRecordRow`: a record event of the same type, rep count, value and session), so it follows the record events and a first session, or an equal weight for more reps, is never badged. A set with no reps (a failed 140 x 0), or a loaded carry with no distance, is an attempt and holds no record. Its Edit opens the exercise editor (phase 4).

**Plates.** `plateLoad` searches in hundredths of the unit shown, so a 135 lb target on 45 lb plates is exact (kilograms would miss by a few thousandths); the count of a plate is both sides together, a single plate of a size can't go on both. It returns the fewest plates that make the closest loadable weight, the lighter on a tie, and `exact: false` when that isn't the target, which the line says in words ("Closest load: 82.5 kg"). The line shows for the plate-loaded equipment (`isPlateLoaded`: `barbell`, `ez_bar`, `trap_bar`), on the open set, from the exercise's own `barKg`, else the equipment's default bar: the inventory's heaviest bar for a barbell, about 10 kg or 25 lb for an EZ bar and about 25 kg or 55 lb for a trap bar (`barKgFor`). An inventory still equal to the metric default, read in pounds, is shown as the pound default (`effectivePlateInventory`): a pound lifter told "20 kg bar" would be told something false. `warmupRamp` (40, 60, 80 percent, rounded to loadable weights) is shown from phase 4 (see below).

**The finish summary** lists the records the workout set, per exercise, capped at three by notability (`summarizeRecords`: estimated 1RM, heaviest weight, best set, rep counts low to high, volume), each with what it beat ("was 80 kg").

**Mood on training days** (`trainingDayMoodContrast`) counts only the app's own finished sessions: a day with no Health workout is ambiguous (nothing recorded and a refused read are one answer), so Health's are never read. A rest day is a day with a mood logged **on or after the first logged workout**: before it the person wasn't using the workout log, and every day would read as a rest day. Both mood-log gates apply (`MIN_PAIRED_DAYS` counted from the first workout, `MIN_CONTRAST_DAYS` each side), a day with no mood is neither side, and the caption says it doesn't show cause and effect. The MCP `mood_insights` returns the same row as `byTrainingDay`, and leaves it empty when Workouts is off or the sessions (health-record data) are withheld from the server.

**Not built in phase 3:** a session detail page behind a history row (built in phase 5, "The workout page"). (The warm-up ramp in the UI and the exercise editor came in phase 4.) Plates for other equipment (a machine, a Smith machine) are not shown: only the three bars above are loaded by hand.

## Phase 4, as built

What phase 4 settled that the plan above left open. Files: `workoutProgression.ts`, `workoutProgram.ts` and `workoutProgramLibrary.ts` in `src/utils/` (each tested 1:1); `ProgramEditorSheet.tsx`, `ProgramLibrarySheet.tsx` and `ExerciseEditorSheet.tsx` in `src/components/`; the Programs view in `WorkoutsScreen.tsx`; the suggestion card (`SuggestionCard`) in `WorkoutModeSheet.tsx`; the warm-up control in `RoutineEditorSheet.tsx`; `addLibraryProgram` and the rotation filing in `useWorkoutStore`; `Task.workoutProgramId`; and `save_program`, `delete_program` and `next_workout` in `mcp/src/workoutTools.ts`. The suggestion card follows the approved `workouts-mocks/workout-mode.png`; the Programs view, the program editor, the library, the warm-up control and the exercise editor were mocked in the phase 4 PR.

**Progression (`proposeNext`):**
- **A proposal is never applied unasked.** Workout mode shows it on the shown exercise's card ("Today 3 × 6–8 at 82.5 kg. Up 2.5 kg because every set last time reached 8.") with one button ("Use 82.5 kg", or "Use these weights" when the sets differ), which writes the weights and reps onto the planned working sets as ordinary edits. Once a working set of the exercise is logged, or the plan already asks for it, the card shows the target plainly with no button: offering it again after it is in use read as a second, different suggestion. Nothing is stored; the proposal is computed from the exercise's history each time.
- **Which scheme:** the routine item's own progression, else the program's scheme when the workout belongs to one (`schemeFor`). Only weight and reps exercises get one.
- **What "last time" is:** the newest earlier session's done working sets (working and AMRAP; warm-ups, drop sets and planned rows never count), the live session excluded. The working weight is the heaviest of them.
- **Rep range (double):** up by the increment once every planned working set reached the top of the range (a set skipped counts as short), aiming for the bottom of the range; otherwise the same weight, aiming for the top.
- **Linear:** up after a workout where every set reached its reps; after `LINEAR_DELOAD_AFTER` (3) short workouts in a row **at the same weight**, the nearest load somebody can make at or under 90% of it (`DELOAD_FRACTION`, rounded down), which can be a little more than 10% off. The reason states the real change ("Down 5 kg after 3 workouts in a row short of 5 reps at 30 kg."), never "about 10%". Where nothing lighter can be loaded (the bare bar, a dumbbell's first increment) it proposes `same` and says it is already the lightest load, rather than a deload of 0. A success, or a different weight, starts the count again.
- **RPE:** the last top set's RPE against the item's target (`DEFAULT_TARGET_RPE` 8 when none): within a point, the same; otherwise 3% of the load per point (`RPE_LOAD_PER_POINT`), moving at least one step, and never down to nothing: a dumbbell, machine or cable floors at one increment, and a load that cannot go lower stays (`same`). With no RPE recorded it keeps the weight and asks for one.
- **Percent:** each working set is its `pctOfTm` of the program's training max for the exercise (65, 75, 85 by position when a target names none), stepped through a wave by the program's week (`waveOffset`: +5 points a week for three weeks, and the last week of a cycle of four or more 10 points under week one). Without a training max the card says to set one. It needs no history.
- **Loads somebody can make.** A barbell, EZ bar or trap bar rounds through `plateLoad` on its own bar, in the unit shown, so a pound gym's steps are exact. A dumbbell, machine or cable steps by the increment from wherever it was (22 kg goes to 24.5, not to 25) and rounds computed loads (a deload, a percentage) to a multiple of it. An increase never rounds back to the same load. The increment is the item's `incrementKg`, else 2.5 kg or 5 lb.

**Programs (`programProgress`):**
- **The week and the next routine are derived, never stored**, from the program's finished sessions on or after its `startedAt`, oldest first. **A week is one pass through the rotation**, not a calendar week: a program moves on only by workouts done, so a week off waits rather than skipping part of the plan. A session of the routine that is next moves on by one; a later routine skips to just after it; a routine the rotation doesn't hold changes nothing. A rotation may name a routine twice.
- **A cycle** is `weeks` passes. When one has just been done (`cycleDone`), a percentage program's card lists the raised training maxes (`trainingMaxRaises`: +2.5 kg, or +5 kg for a lift whose primary muscles include the quads, hamstrings or glutes; 5 and 10 lb) and offers "Start next cycle", which writes them and sets `startedAt` to now. That reset is what keeps the offer from coming back: the count starts again, so nothing has to remember that it was accepted. Doing the next workout without accepting it carries on with the old maxes. Over MCP the same reset is `save_program`'s `startNextCycle: true` (it needs the program's `id` and writes `startedAt = now` through `updateProgram`, the store path the card uses); `next_workout`'s note points at it when a cycle has just ended. Raising the maxes alone leaves `cycleDone` true, so the phone would offer a second raise.
- **A rotation files its routines under the program** (`WorkoutRoutine.programId`) when they belong to none, and frees the ones taken out of it, in `addProgram`/`updateProgram`, so the app's editor and `save_program` agree, and a routine started on its own from the Routines list still counts toward its program.
- **The Programs view** is a fourth segment on the Workouts screen. Each card says the week, the scheme and the next routine, with "Start" for that routine; tapping the card opens the program editor (`EditorSheet`, full screen, since the rotation drags). The editor shows where the program is (read-only), the rotation, the scheme for exercises whose routine chooses none (tap the chosen one again to clear it, and workout mode then proposes nothing for those exercises), the weeks in a cycle ("No end" for open-ended) and, for exercises moved by percentages (`percentExercises`), a training max each, with a proposal of 90% of the best estimated 1RM (`suggestedTrainingMaxKg`) offered as a "Use" button, never filled in.
- **The library** (`PROGRAM_LIBRARY`): "5×5 linear", "Push / Pull / Legs", "Upper / Lower" and "4-week percentage wave", generic names only, built from `lib:` exercises. Adding one writes ordinary routines and a program in one transaction (`addLibraryProgram`) and opens its editor; nothing refers back to the library afterwards. Adding the same one again suffixes any routine or program name already held ("Upper A (2)", `freeWorkoutName`, on `exerciseNameKey`), and the sheet ignores a second tap on Add. A bodyweight or timed exercise in one carries no progression, and the barbell lifts warm up with a ramp.
- **The task link** (`Task.workoutProgramId`): the row's Workout starts the program's next routine at the moment of the tap (`useStartWorkout`), and finishing completes the task the way a routine link does. The TaskEditor's Workout row is one field with programs and routines in two groups; picking either clears the other. No template seeds it, for the routine link's reason (`templateItemParity.test.ts`).

**Warm-up ramp:** the routine editor's warm-up is a Ramp warm-ups switch (weight and reps exercises), or warm-up sets of the item's own in its set table; adding a warm-up turns the ramp off, and turning the ramp on drops them. A ramp is `WorkoutRoutineItem.warmupRamp` with no warm-up targets of its own; `startSession` lays out `rampSteps` (40, 60 and 80 percent, as loads) from the first working weight it lays out, as planned sets the person edits like any other, and using a suggestion works the planned warm-ups out again from the new weight. Each planned warm-up takes the ramp step at its place among all the exercise's warm-ups, done ones included, so after the first is logged "Use" does not start the ramp over from its lightest. A percentage program's first session has no weight to ramp to when the workout starts, so it lays out no warm-ups; "Use" then adds the ramp's warm-ups, planned, ahead of the working sets (`rampWarmupsToAdd`, `addPlannedWarmups`), only when the exercise has no warm-up row at all. A ramp is for weight and reps exercises only: `addRoutine`/`updateRoutine` refuse it (`rampKind`) on any other kind of exercise, and `save_routine` says so by name. The set table's ramp row shows the loads for the target weight. The key is written only when on, so an item from before it reads unchanged.

**The exercise editor** (`ExerciseEditorSheet`), from the Edit button on the exercise page and nested inside that sheet's Modal: for a custom exercise the name, what a set records, equipment, main and helping muscles, bar weight (plate-loaded equipment only, with the default bar as its empty value) and notes; for a built-in only the name, notes and bar weight, through `updateExercise`, which enforces the same split and refuses a taken name. It also refuses to change a custom exercise's kind once any set is logged with it on this device (`hasHistory`, the same limit `deleteExercise` has for other devices' sets): the editor dims the kind control and says "Kind can’t change once sets are logged with this exercise." Equipment and muscles stay editable. A page sheet with staged state, so Cancel and the swipe-down go through `confirmDiscard`.

**MCP:** `save_program` and `delete_program` (with `WRITE_TOOLS`, `COUNTERPARTS` and Activity entries under the subject `program`), `list_workouts`' programs with their derived week and next routine, and `next_workout`, a separate read rather than a field on `exercise_history`, because the question it answers ("what do I lift next time") is about every exercise of the next routine at once: one call instead of one per exercise. It rounds against the person's plates and unit (`workoutLoads`) and returns kilograms beside the phone's own words. `instructions.ts` says a progression is a proposal the person confirms, weights are kg, and a session the person didn't do is never written. Without Include health logs the replica has no sessions, so a program reads as on its first routine and the history-based proposals are missing; the tool says so.

**Demo:** the demo's program is "Upper / Lower" added from the library, with Upper A and Lower A edited into the person's own; the task "Lift" names the program, and its next routine (Lower A) opens on a squat that warms up with a ramp and suggests going up 5 kg. "Leg day" keeps a routine link, later in the week.

**The current program** (`currentProgram`, `src/utils/workoutProgram.ts`): the program "my workout" (Siri, the watch's "From Today"), `next_workout` and the Workouts screen's Start menu default to when several are in use. It is a pointer, the `currentProgramId` setting (device-synced, shaped like `defaultGymId`), not a flag on each program, so two can never both be current, and it carries no progress, which stays derived. It is **read, never trusted**: a pick that was archived, emptied or deleted is passed over rather than cleaned up, so there is no sweep or migration. With no usable pick, exactly one program in use is current by itself, so nobody with one program sets anything, and several with no pick have none: Siri opens the Workouts screen and `next_workout` asks for a `programId`, rather than guessing. Doing a workout from another program deliberately does not make it current; the app does not move the pick under the person. "Current" and "Make current" show only with two or more programs to choose between.

## Phase 7, as built

Files: `src/utils/workoutCsv.ts` (pure), `importSessions` in `useWorkoutStore`, `WorkoutImportSheet.tsx`, and the Workouts screen's "…" menu. It reuses `pickBackupFile` (the document picker) and `CsvExportSheet` with `writeExportFile`/`shareCsvFile` (the mood and food log exports). No native dependency and no network call. Nothing was added to the MCP server: the file is on the person's phone, and the sessions an import adds are read through the existing workout tools.

- **Headers, never positions.** Strong has four layouts and Hevy one, and a header is matched case-insensitively with punctuation ignored (`Weight (kg)`, `weight_kg`), an optional column that is missing reads as empty and an extra one is ignored. Strong S1 (iOS, comma, 12 columns, no unit column anywhere), S2 (Android, semicolon, `Weight Unit` of `lbs` or blank for kg, `Distance Unit` of `mi.` or `km`, text quoted with the inner quotes **not** doubled, a `Workout Duration`), S3 (semicolon, every cell quoted, units in the headers: `Duration (sec)`, `Weight (kg)`, `Distance (meters)`) and S4 (S2 without RPE or a workout duration). Hevy is `title, start_time, end_time, description, exercise_title, superset_id, exercise_notes, set_index, set_type, weight_kg|weight_lbs, reps, distance_km|distance_miles, duration_seconds, rpe`. A comma decimal (`82,5`) is read only in a semicolon file; in a comma file a comma inside a number is a thousands separator. The CSV reader is lenient about a quote that isn't followed by a delimiter or line end inside a quoted field, because S2 doesn't double them.
- **Kilograms at the edge.** Pounds and miles are converted while reading, so nothing past `parseWorkoutCsv` knows the file's unit. A file with weights and no unit anywhere (S1) is read in the person's own unit and says so (`unitAssumed`); the review sheet then asks, and re-reads on the answer. S1's distance has no unit either, so it follows that answer (kilograms means kilometers, pounds means miles), and a file with a distance and no weight asks too. A unit's trailing period is stripped (`mi.`).
- **Dates are local wall-clock times, built from their parts.** Neither app writes a zone. Strong is `yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm:ss`, or a 12-hour `2025-01-30 12:34:56 pm` (the space before am/pm may be a no-break or narrow no-break space, any case). Hevy is day first (`26 Feb 2024, 08:00`, zero-padded or not) or month first and 12-hour (`Oct 1, 2026, 8:28 PM`). A value that carries `Z` or an offset is honored as that instant. An unknown or impossible date (`3/4/2024`, Feb 31, 25:00) is a skipped row, never a guess; a time inside a DST gap is accepted and lands on the shifted instant, since the person really wrote it.
- **Set markers.** Strong's `W`, `D` and `F` and Hevy's `warmup`, `dropset` and `failure` map to warm-up, drop and amrap (to failure). A rest-timer row, and a set with no weight, reps, time or distance, are skipped. A Strong `Note` row is an exercise note: the model keeps notes on sets, so its text goes on that exercise's first set in the workout (ahead of the set's own note) and the row is not a set. The sheet counts and names every reason a row was left out (`describeSkipped`): rest timers, empty sets, unreadable dates, rows with no exercise, and notes with nothing to attach to. A weight of 0 is no load, not a weight of zero. Hevy's sets are ordered by `set_index`; Strong's by the rows.
- **Idempotent with no new column.** An imported session's id is a hash of the source and the file's own date text (`importSessionId`), not the instant it computes to and not the title, so the same file read in another time zone, or re-exported after a rename, names the same ids. A session already in the log is skipped too when it started at the same time (`ExistingIndex`), whatever it is called: to the second, or to the minute for Hevy, which writes no seconds. That is what keeps the app's own export from doubling every workout, since the export truncates to whole seconds while a session keeps its milliseconds. The store checks again when it writes, so a plan made before a sync landed can't double a workout.
- **Only an exact name or the same words in any order is decided by the app** (`matchExerciseName`): the exact name key (archived exercises included, since a custom name is unique even archived), then the same words with the equipment in brackets counted as words ("Bench Press (Barbell)" is "Barbell bench press"). Everything fuzzier is a `suggest`, never an auto-match: a name one letter out once its words are joined ("Back Squat" against "Hack squat") or sharing most of its words ("Decline Bench Press (Smith Machine)" against "Smith machine bench press") is usually a different exercise, and the answer is about to become history. Nothing is remembered between imports: only what the person confirms on the sheet is written, and the matching is the same each time.
- **The review sheet shows every name, not only the unmatched ones.** The auto-matched names are listed with a Change button. A name with only a suggestion sits under "Needs a choice" with its closest match shown and a "Use this" button (and "Use all suggestions" for several), so confirming is one tap; the suggestion is not applied until it is tapped. Unmatched names each need a choice (Match opens `ExercisePickerSheet`, Create, Skip) before Import enables, with Create all and Skip all for a long list. Only workouts not already in the log count toward the names that need a decision. A created exercise takes the file's own spelling, the kind its sets imply (`inferExerciseKind`) and the equipment its words name (`guessEquipment`), with no muscles; it is editable like any custom exercise.
- **One transaction.** `importSessions` writes the custom exercises, sessions and sets in one `dbTransaction`, then updates state. The exercise picker is rendered inside the sheet's own Modal (two sibling sheets can't both present), and Cancel and the swipe-down go through `confirmDiscard` once any choice is staged.
- **Demo mode.** The import is not gated: it writes to the demo database like any other change there and goes with it. It touches nothing outside SQLite.
- **A finished workout can become a routine** (`routineItemsFromSets`, the workout page's Save as routine row). It is how an import's history turns into routines, and it works on any workout. Each exercise becomes an item in the order done, with a target per ticked set at the weight and reps lifted. Warm-ups are left out (a ramp or the last warm-up is laid out again at the start, and an exercise only warmed up has nothing to build on), unticked sets are plans and are ignored, and supersets are kept. Rest and progression are not carried over because the workout never chose them. The session keeps no link to the routine it made.
- **Export** (`workoutExportCsv`): finished workouts only, oldest first, one row per set done, in Strong's columns so the file opens as it is and `parseWorkoutCsv` reads it back. Weights are in the unit shown with the unit in the header (`Weight (lb)`), distance is meters, and Set Order counts non-warm-up sets from 1 with W, D or F in place of a number. Nothing is derived (no volume, no estimated 1RM). The range picker is `CsvExportSheet`.

- **Save is the trigger.** `finishSession` (workout mode's "Save workout") writes the session as `.traditionalStrengthTraining` over its own start and end, with no energy and no metadata of its own, while Log to Health, its new Workouts row (`workoutHealthWrite`, off by default) and the Workouts area are all on. - **Retraction.** `deleteSession` reads the id off the row and retracts it, whatever the switches say. `updateSession` rewrites only a written workout whose start or end moved, and only while writing is on; a title, notes, effort or `replaceSets` change touches nothing Health holds, and a workout never written is never written by an edit. Over MCP, a workout marked `inAppleHealth` can't have its times changed or be deleted, the food tools' refusal.

Changing a time is what rewrites the workout in Health, and the line saying so shows only while it holds (an id, and writing on). The page refuses a finish in the future, a finish at or before the start, and, for a workout in Health while writing is on, a span over a day: Health would take it back out and refuse it again with nothing said. **The background.** A cold launch woken by a watch tap never read the step timers back (only `App.tsx` hydrates them), so a rest started there would have been written over the stored stack, a recipe's timer included: the drain hydrates the timers before any workout tap applies, and every read of a session's rest goes through `restFor`, which reads them back first, so a launch that ticked nothing still sends the watch the rest that is counting down. And a Health write fired in the background can outlive the time borrowed for it (the snapshot write hands it back), which would leave a workout in Health with no id on the row to retract it by: a finish or a watch workout applied in the background is flagged pending instead (`deferHealthWrite`, only while writing is on), and the next pass in front writes it, as it does an agent's workout.

**The rest on the watch:**
- **Counted from stamps**, never a running timer. On the phone's workout it is the phone's rest as the snapshot has it, with the watch's own taps laid over it in order until the phone answers (a set's own rest from its tap's moment, +30 s, Skip).
- **Shown only while it is about the newest set done.** A superset partner's set leaves the rest going on the phone (-1), and that older rest belongs to a set already moved past.
- **Rest is over** turns orange and counts up with "Go to the set", which only puts the rest away. **Nothing moves on by itself.**
- **The wrist.** A haptic when it ends with the app in front (`WKInterfaceDevice.play(.notification)`). On the watch's own workout, a local notification at the end too (`UNUserNotificationCenter`, asked for at the first start; refused, it is the haptic alone), never inside quiet hours, the phone's rest rule. On the phone's workout the alert is the phone's own rest notification, which reaches the wrist while the phone is locked, except for a set done with the phone out of reach, which the phone doesn't hear of in time: the watch rings for that one itself.

**The clash: "Keep both, ask once."** A watch workout whose span overlaps another session's (touching ends don't count; a workout still going spans to now) is saved anyway, and the pair is kept in the settings table (`watchWorkoutClashes`, device-local, so it survives the app being closed and a background launch) until the phone is in front. `useWatchClashPrompt` (app-level, since a frozen tab runs no effects) asks one pair at a time: "Keep both workouts?", a body naming both, with the day when it isn't today ("Your Apple Watch saved “Push A” from 6:02 PM to 6:54 PM. Your iPhone has “Pull A” from 6:05 PM to 6:50 PM. Keep both, or delete the one you don’t want."), adding "Deleting a workout also takes it out of Apple Health." only while either is in Health, and the buttons "Keep both", "Delete the watch’s" and "Delete the iPhone’s". Deleting goes through `deleteSession`, so Health is retracted. A pair is asked once: it leaves the list when answered, a watch workout delivered twice is written (and compared) once, and a pair one of whose workouts has gone since is dropped unasked.

**Demo mode** reaches none of it: the watch write, the drain and every queue go through `widgetBridge()`, which is null in demo mode, and the clash store reloads with the database swap. Nothing is seeded, since nothing on the watch can be shown in a demo.

**Where it differs from the mock:**
- Finish workout asks first, with what finishing does ("12 sets done. Sets you haven't done are left off."); a workout of the watch's own with nothing done says nothing is saved and ends without sending anything.
- Every set done shows "All sets done" (or "More sets on your iPhone" past the cap) with a pointer to Finish, a state the mock doesn't draw.
- The top bar's time is the system clock the watch always draws there, not the mock's elapsed time; the finished screen gives the length.
- Skip on the rest reads "Skip", as drawn, where the phone's footer and Lock Screen say "Skip rest" (its accessibility label says "Skip rest").
- The load card shows on every set of the exercise still to do, warm-ups included, until a working set is done: workout mode's rule, where the mock draws it on the first working set.
- A swipe shows only the moves there are: the first exercise has no move up, the last no move down.
- Reopening the Workout row after Finish on the phone's workout, before the phone has applied it, says "Push A is finished. It moves to your iPhone the next time it's nearby." rather than the empty start list's "Add a routine…".
- Add exercise leaves out what the workout already has; Search shows the words dictated in its place, with "Clear search" under it; and the list says so when it is empty ("Exercises you've logged in the last 90 days show here.") or nothing matches.

**Not verified without a device:** no Swift compiles here; every declaration was checked against Apple's JSON docs, and the build is the first real test. Beyond that: the Crown turning a focused box inside a vertical-page `TabView` (a swipe still pages; the Crown should stay with the box), whether the iPhone's forwarded rest notification and the watch's in-app haptic both fire with the watch app in front, notification authorization on a watch whose iPhone already granted it, and the `dumbbell.fill` symbol (a wrong name draws nothing rather than failing the build). From the additions: whether `.buttonStyle(.plain)` styles a `TextFieldLink` (its docs call it a button interface without saying a button style applies; if it doesn't, the pencil gets the default button's background around its circle), the set screen with the load card fitting a 41 mm watch without scrolling, and swipe actions on a list row inside the vertical-page `TabView`.

## Phase 9, as built

What the watch's second phase (W2, an `HKWorkoutSession` on the watch) settled. `health-data.md`'s "Heart rate during a workout" is the review it was built against, written first. Files: `targets/todo-watch/WatchHealthSession.swift` (the session, the builder, the save, the retraction, crash recovery, and the store's side of it), the Health fields on the watch's section and taps (`WatchWorkout.swift`, `WatchTaps.swift`, `WatchStore.swift`) and the heart-rate line in `WatchWorkoutScreens.swift`; `plugins/withWatchApp.js` (the entitlement, the watch's own usage strings, `WKBackgroundModes` and `UIBackgroundModes`); on the phone, `src/utils/heartRateZones.ts` (the zones, time in zones, the maximum and its label; tested), `src/utils/watchHealthWorkout.ts` (what the phone asks of the watch; tested) and `src/store/useWatchHealthStore.ts`, the hold-off and take-over in `useWorkoutStore` (`markWatchMeasuring`, `recordWatchHealthWorkout`, `recordHealthWrite`), `WATCH_WORKOUT_WAIT_MINUTES` in `healthWorkoutSync.ts`, the three tap kinds in `widgetQuietTaps.ts`, `readWorkoutHeartRate` in the Health bridge, `src/hooks/useWorkoutHeartRate.ts`, `WorkoutHeartRateCard.tsx`, the card and the retime line in `WorkoutDetailSheet`, and `MaxHeartRateSheet.tsx` with its row in Settings › Workouts. Built to the approved mock (`workouts-mocks/heart-rate.png`), with each zone row's range added ("Zone 2 · 112–130").

**When the watch measures.**
- **Whenever it runs a workout, its own or the phone's, while the phone says workouts go to Health.** The section carries `healthWrite`, which is `workoutHealthWriteOn()`: the three switches phase 5 already has (Log to Health, its Workouts row, the Workouts area), and no new one. Off, the watch runs a workout as phase 8 did, with no session. Turned off mid-workout, the session is thrown away unsaved.
- **The watch's own workout is measured from Start. The phone's is measured from the moment its workout screen is opened on the watch** (`WorkoutSessionView` calls `openedWorkoutScreen`), which is Apple's guidance for a workout started on the iPhone, and the watch's workout takes the phone workout's start (`beginCollection(withStart:)`), so its span is the phone's even though readings begin when the watch joined. Opening the app, or the phone's workout showing on Today, starts nothing, and nor does a launch in the background to take a snapshot. Once measuring it carries on wherever the screens go, and a session taken back after a crash counts as opened.
- **One session at a time is the cost, and why it waits for the screen.** The watch runs one workout session across every app: starting this one ends another app's (it gets `errorAnotherWorkoutSessionStarted` and ends), and another app starting one ends this. Measuring the phone's workout whenever the app was open would end, say, Apple's Workout app the moment the person glanced at their set. A workout whose session fails here is never started again that launch (`refusedMeasurements`, for any failure, not only that one, since every failure ends the session and a restart could end someone else's), and the phone is told measuring stopped, so it writes its own copy without heart rate.
- **A stray start can't run on.** A routine row on the watch's Start list asks "Start X?" before it begins (`WorkoutStartView`), and a workout started there with no set done for `idleOwnWorkoutLimit` (30 minutes) ends by itself and saves nothing, the same as Finish with nothing done (`endIdleOwnWorkout`, run on a timer from Start and on every `syncHealthSession`). The session is what makes it matter: it counts every minute as exercise in Health, so a forgotten one read as hours of exercise and pre-picked the mood sheet's "Worked out" tag. One set done makes it the person's, and it stays until they finish it. The phone's counterpart is `discardIdleSession` (`isIdleLiveSession`, `IDLE_SESSION_MS` in `workoutSession.ts`, the same 30 minutes), a launch-time pass in `retentionPasses`; change the two figures together. The workout screen also has a close button in its top bar (`WorkoutSessionView`), because Finish is a page below the set that nothing on the set mentions, and a workout started by mistake looked impossible to end without logging a set.
- **What it measures:** `.traditionalStrengthTraining`, indoor, through `HKLiveWorkoutDataSource`, which collects heart rate and active energy, and the app stays in front on the wrist through a rest (the `workout-processing` background mode). The watch asks for Health access itself, the first time it starts measuring: share the workout and active energy, read heart rate and active energy. Without workout sharing allowed there, nothing is measured.
- **Live heart rate** is the builder's most recent reading, under the top bar on the set and rest screens: "♥ 142 bpm" in one red, whatever the number. No zone color, no word about it.

**Ending.** Apple's order: `stopActivity`, `.stopped`, `endCollection`, `finishWorkout`, `end()` (with a five-second fallback should `.stopped` never arrive).
- **Finish on the watch's own workout** saves first, then sends the workout to the phone carrying `health: { workoutId }` (an empty object for a save made with the watch locked, which has no id). The workout's file is kept until it is sent. **With nothing done it is discarded** (`discardWorkout()`, then `end()`), and nothing is sent, as before.
- **Finish on the phone's workout** saves at that moment, and a `healthWorkout` tap (session and id) follows the `finish` tap.
- **The phone finishing (or deleting) its workout** reaches the watch as the workout leaving the snapshot. Saved, ending when the phone's did (`finished`, the phone's workouts ended in the last day), unless the phone deleted it (`dropSessions`) or writing went off, which throw it away.
- **A session that fails** (or a save that does) sends `measuring` with `on: false`, so the phone writes its own as Save would. So does a measurement thrown away because writing went off on the phone, so the row stops waiting for the watch.
- **A crash mid-workout**: the app delegate's `handleActiveWorkoutRecovery` takes the session back (`recoverActiveWorkoutSession`) and sets its data source and delegates up again; the workout it was for is kept in the watch's App Group (`watch_health_session.json`). Nothing starts while the system is answering, a recovered session the app can't place is ended unsaved, and the store then matches it to what is going on (a phone workout finished meanwhile is saved). Each save callback tears down only the session it was saving.
- **A save made with the watch locked** comes back with no workout (`finishWorkout`'s documented nil), so there is no id and no times to send. The row ends up `measuredOnWatch` with no Health id and no pending flag: the workout is in Health, the page shows no heart-rate card (it reads by id), a retime or delete has nothing to retract, and the phone never writes a copy. The page says nothing about it, because nothing it could say is certain: the same fields also describe a workout whose watch stopped answering with writing off.
- **The rest's end with the wrist down.** While a session runs for the workout, the watch taps the wrist itself when a rest ends (`refreshRestAlarm`), in place of the notification it would otherwise have scheduled (`restBuzz`), and under the same quiet hours, so it never taps twice. The phone's own rest notification, for the phone's workout in reach, is unchanged. `play(_:)` works in the background only for "apps with an active workout session", and Apple's workout guide asks for the audio background mode for haptics during one, so the watch's Info.plist has `UIBackgroundModes` `audio`. The same page says HealthKit "stops gathering heart rate data until after the haptic engine finishes", so each buzz leaves a moment's gap in the readings. A session that stops before the rest ends puts the notification back.

**One copy in Health.** The phone never writes a second copy of a workout the watch saved:
- **`WorkoutSession.measuredOnWatch`** (column `measured_on_watch`, synced with `healthWorkoutId`, part of the health record like the rest of the row): the watch measured this workout, and the row's Health id is, or will be, the watch's. Set by the watch's `measuring` tap (the phone's workout, as the watch starts) or by a watch workout arriving with `health`. It is a column rather than derived from the Health workout having heart rate, because the workout page has to warn about a retime with Read Apple Health off, and a delete has to know which device to ask, neither of which can wait on a read that may be refused.
- **Save holds back for it.** `writeToHealth` defers a measured session's write to the pending pass (`healthWritePending`), and `pendingWorkoutWriteAction` waits until `WATCH_WORKOUT_WAIT_MINUTES` (30) after the workout ended before writing the phone's own. The number is argued on the constant: the watch normally answers in seconds, and the wait covers a watch carried out of reach, a transfer delivered at watchOS's pace, or a watch that died; a wrong guess costs nothing, since a late watch workout still takes over. A workout already holding an id writes nothing.
- **Take-over.** A watch workout that arrives after the phone wrote its copy replaces it, and the phone retracts its own (`recordWatchHealthWorkout`); a phone write that lands after the watch's arrived is retracted (`recordHealthWrite`). Applied in the background (a watch tap that woke the phone), a tap that needs the phone's own HealthKit (a retract, or the rewrite below) is left for a drain in front (`watchWorkoutTapTiming`), since HealthKit refuses a delete with the phone locked and a save made locked returns no id.
- **A copy out of date for the row** (`watchCopyStale`). The `healthWorkout` tap carries the times the watch saved at. The copy is out of date when its start differs from the row's by more than `WATCH_SPAN_TOLERANCE_MS` (2 s, the clocks' rounding), or its end does and either somebody edited the times or the gap is past `WATCH_FINISH_SKEW_MS`. Out of date, it ends as a retime does: the phone writes its own at the row's times and the watch retracts its copy once that lands. A copy the phone already wrote at those times is kept, and the watch's retracted. With writing off, or Health refusing, the watch's stays at its old times.
- **A Finish tapped on both devices keeps the watch's copy.** The one way the ends part without an edit: the phone's Finish reaches the watch in the snapshot seconds later, and a Finish tapped on the watch before then saves at its own moment while the phone keeps the first (either can be the later). An end off by up to `WATCH_FINISH_SKEW_MS` (5 minutes) on a row nobody retimed is that, so the watch's copy is taken as the session's Health workout, heart rate and all, and the row keeps its own end: nothing is rewritten. The bound is argued on the constant: both taps come at the end of the same workout, seconds to a minute or two apart, and past about one long rest between heavy sets the kept copy would misstate how long the workout took.
- **Whether the times were edited is recorded, because nothing else says it.** `WorkoutSession.timesEdited` (column `times_edited`, synced with the row in the health record, in backups with the rest of the table) is set by `updateSession` when the start moves, or the end moves once there was one. That one action is both the workout page's Started and Finished rows and the MCP server's `update_workout`, so an agent's retime reaches the phone on the row itself. `finishSession` writes the first end through the same action, and that is the finish, not an edit. Never cleared. Deriving it failed: `updated_at` moves on every Health stamp and set, and no other field on the row or its sets says which end a person chose.
- **The watch's workout carries no sync identifier.** The builder could carry one (`addMetadata`), but whether HealthKit matches a sync identifier across two sources (the iPhone app and its watch app) is not documented, and under the reading where it does, a late phone write would replace the measured workout and the take-over would then retract the replacement, leaving Health with neither. The ordering above holds the one copy instead.

**Delete and retime.**
- **The watch retracts what it saved.** HealthKit lets an app delete only what it saved, and nothing Apple publishes says whether an iPhone app counts as the saver of what its watch app saved (checked: the delete pages, `HKSource`, the HealthKit guides). So the phone never tries. `deleteSession` puts the id on `retractWorkouts`, which the watch reads from the section, deletes (the workout, and the active energy saved with it, found through `predicateForObjects(from:)` while the workout is still there), and answers with a `healthRetracted` tap, which takes it off the list. A measurement of a workout deleted before the watch saved it is put on `dropSessions` instead, and thrown away. Heart-rate readings stay in Health as readings: this app never shares heart rate, so it may not delete it. A watch workout arriving for a session the phone has deleted is retracted the same way. The workout page's delete alert says so: "Your Apple Watch, which saved it, also removes it from Apple Health." The lists are device-local (`watchHealthRequests`), so **a delete made on another device** reaches the watch through the phone: the sync apply reads each deleted session's `health_workout_id` and `measured_on_watch` before the row goes (`ApplyReport.removedWatchWorkouts`), and the reload after the sync asks the watch exactly as a delete here does (`askWatchAboutSyncedDeletes`, refused in demo mode). The watch answers only for what it dealt with: a workout deleted, or already gone (nothing matched, `errorNoData`). Anything else (`errorDatabaseInaccessible`, the watch locked on its charger) stays on the list and is tried again with the next snapshot and when the app comes to the front.
- **A retime rewrites it as the phone's own**, writing the new times from the phone, which carries no heart rate or energy, and only then asking the watch to retract its copy (`recordHealthWrite`'s take-over); the row's `measuredOnWatch` goes false. Never retracted first: the watch's has no sync identifier for the phone's write to replace, so a rewrite Health refused would leave neither. Refused, the watch's stays at the old times. A rewrite saved with the phone locked has no id to keep, and the watch still retracts its copy. The workout page's line under the times says so before the change, for a measured workout in Health or one still waiting for the watch's copy: "Changing the times also changes the workout in Apple Health, and it loses its heart rate and energy."

**The heart-rate card** (`WorkoutDetailSheet`, after the stat tiles):
- **Read for one workout, each time the page opens** (and on foreground, and when its Health id changes), only for a measured workout with an id, only while Read Apple Health is on, never in demo mode. The bridge finds the workout by id and reads the heart-rate samples added to it (`predicateForObjects(from: workout)`), not every reading in its span: a watch worn all day records heart rate in the background, and dividing those into zones would be a measurement nobody started. The average and maximum are the workout's own statistics (`statistics(for:)`, `averageQuantity()`, `maximumQuantity()`), else worked out from the readings.
- **Shown only when Health returns heart rate for that workout.** A refused read and no data are one answer, so no card and no "no heart rate" line.
- **Zones:** five at 50, 60, 70, 80 and 90 percent of the maximum, in whole beats ("Zone 2 · 112–130", and zone 5 open at the top, "Zone 5 · 168+"), zone 5 first, every bar the accent on one scale (the time measured in any zone). A reading stands until the next one for at most a minute (`MAX_READING_GAP_SECONDS`); a longer silence is in no zone, as is a reading below zone 1.
- **Series samples.** A heart-rate sample can hold a series (`HKQuantitySample.count` above 1), and then its quantity is their average. The bridge sends each sample's start, end, count and highest reading (`HKDiscreteQuantitySample.maximumQuantity`), and the series' average is spread over its own span, at most a minute per gap between its readings, then held after it under the same rule. The average weighs each sample by its count, and the maximum takes each series' own highest. The readings inside are not walked (`HKQuantitySeriesSampleQuery`): what the watch's session stores is unknown until a device shows it.
- **The maximum** is the typed `maxHeartRate`, else 220 minus the age from the year of birth (Settings › Apple Health › About you; the calorie budget reads the same `bodyProfile.birthYear`), else none (no zones: the two figures and one line naming the two settings). Both are synced and in the health record (`bodyProfile`, the year of birth's setting, since round two), so the zones are the same on every device sharing the health logs. The line naming the maximum's basis shows only when zones are drawn, and is plain `Text`, not `HelpText`, because it is the estimate's label.
- **The setting** is a row in Settings › Workouts whose value reads "186, from age", "190" or "Not set", opening a `CardSheet` with a number field: an empty field goes back to the estimate, and a number outside 100 to 230 is refused in the card with the reason.

**Demo mode** reaches none of it: the read goes through `healthBridge()`, the watch hears nothing (`widgetBridge()`), and the retract and drop lists refuse in demo mode. Nothing is seeded, since neither heart rate nor the watch can be shown in a demo.

**MCP:** heart rate is not visible to the server, which reads no Health. `inAppleHealth` now covers a workout the watch saved, so the instructions say it was written "by the phone or the person's Apple Watch".

**Not verified without a device:** no Swift compiles here; every declaration was checked against Apple's JSON docs and the first build is the real test. Beyond that: whether HealthKit allows a watch app's session to start (the authorization sheet, `startActivity`) as soon as the app comes to the front with the phone's workout already going; whether a builder started with a past start date (the phone workout's) saves a workout spanning it; whether the samples the live data source collects are the ones `predicateForObjects(from:)` returns on the phone once Health syncs the workout across (if not, the card never shows, which is the safe failure); whether the watch can find its own saved workout to delete its energy without asking to read workouts (if not, the energy stays and the workout is still deleted); whether `handleActiveWorkoutRecovery` reaches the SwiftUI app through `WKApplicationDelegateAdaptor` after a crash; and the set screen with the heart-rate line on a 41 mm watch without scrolling. From round two: whether the watch's session stores heart rate as series samples at all, and what their count and span look like (the zones spread a series' average; walking it with `HKQuantitySeriesSampleQuery` is the fallback if the spread is visibly wrong); whether `play(_:)` taps the wrist with the wrist down during a session with the audio mode declared, and how long each tap's gap in the heart-rate readings is; whether App Review questions an audio background mode on an app that plays no sound; whether a `DispatchQueue.main.asyncAfter` fires on time in the background during a session (the buzz's timer); and whether `errorAnotherWorkoutSessionStarted` reaches `didFailWithError` when another app starts a session.

## Per-set routine editing, as built

What the routine editor's set table settled. Files: `RoutineSetTable.tsx` and `RoutineEditorSheet.tsx` in `src/components/`, the per-set edits in `workoutRoutine.ts`, and `setBadge`/`setName` in `workoutSession.ts`. Built to the approved mock (`workouts-mocks/routine-sets.png`, option A).

- **A routine's sets are a table, one row per target**, labelled the way workout mode numbers a session (`routineSetLabels`, over `setBadge`): W, D and F lettered, working sets numbered. The data model already held per-set targets; the editor used to show only a working-set count and one rep range, and wrote that range onto every set but the warm-ups. Now every edit names its set.
- **Nothing in the table expands.** Tapping a set's badge opens its type, Duplicate and Remove; the values are fields typed in place, as workout mode's are (`CellInput`; `parseSetCell`, and `parseRepsTarget` for "8", "8-12" and "5+"). A blank field is "from last time" and its placeholder says so. A small card with a stepper per value was built first and removed: it cost a tap to open and another to close for what is one number. An expanding panel under the tapped row was mocked before that and rejected: it pushed the rest of the table away from the set being changed.
- **Two rules keep the table's shape.** Warm-ups always come first (making a set a warm-up moves it up), and an item keeps at least one set that isn't a warm-up, since a ramp, the summary line and the proposals read an item's working weight and reps off one. The editor doesn't offer an edit that would break either (`canRemoveSet`, `kindsFor`), and the functions refuse one anyway.
- **"To failure" is the existing `amrap` kind**, renamed in the app and lettered F (the mark Strong and Hevy use, and that the CSV export already wrote). Its reps cell reads "6+" for a floor or "Max" for none, and a range becomes its low end when a set is made one. It still counts as a working set for records and for the next-load proposal. Workout mode, the workout page, the Lock Screen, the rest wording and the watch letter it F where they used to number it.
- **No cool-down kind.** It was asked about and left out: strength apps don't have one (a cool-down is cardio or stretching), and a lighter back-off set after the heavy ones is a working set at a lower weight, which per-set weights now write.
- **Add set** copies the last working set and places it after the last working set, so it takes the next number and a drop set or set to failure stays at the end. **Warm-up** adds one after the warm-ups (and turns a ramp off).
- **Each exercise is a card of its own**, with 12pt between, and a superset's cards close up into one block with squared inner corners. The gap is padding inside the row, so `SortableList` measures it as part of the row. Labels sit beside or above their one control; the steppers that used to share a row moved into the per-set cards.

## Risks and open questions

- **A rest that ends in quiet hours makes no sound with the app closed.** That is the decision above. If it bites at a 6 AM session, the fix is to make the setting per subject, not to flip the default.
- **Heart rate is a new kind of read.** Answered in phase 9 by `health-data.md`'s "Heart rate during a workout", written before the code: shown and never interpreted, read only for a workout the watch measured, and the zones' maximum a written carve-out. HRV stays ruled out (resting heart rate has its own display-only carve-out, see `health-data.md`).
- **Which device may retract the watch's workout.** Not documented. The phone asks the watch and never tries itself, so a watch that never runs the app again leaves its workout in Health. A delete made on an iPad reaches the phone by sync, which asks its watch, so it waits for the phone to sync and the watch to hear ("Phase 9, as built").
- **The 5-minute bound for a Finish tapped on both devices** is a guess at how far apart two taps at the end of one workout land. Too short, a slow watch's copy is rewritten and loses its heart rate, as before this rule; too long, Health keeps a copy whose length is off by that much. An edit of any size is always a rewrite, whatever the bound.
- **One workout session at a time, across apps.** Measuring the phone's workout ends any other workout app's session, which is why it waits for the workout screen to be opened on the watch; a person who opens it while running Apple's Workout app ends that one. Another app starting a session ends this one, and the workout is then not measured again that launch.
- **The phone's 30-minute wait** is a guess at how long a watch's workout can take to arrive. Too short, the phone writes a copy the watch's then replaces; too long, a workout whose watch died waits that long for Health. Neither loses a workout.
- **Live Activity churn.** Answered in phase 6 by updating the activity in place rather than restarting it. The update budget applies to push updates, and these all come from the app while it runs, so it shouldn't bite; a device build is still the first real test of how the Lock Screen takes an update on every weight stepped.
- **Watch-first sessions.** Built in phase 8: the routine travels with its plan (last time's numbers and the warm-up ramp), and the workout runs on the watch alone. What it still can't do there: start the workout going on the phone from the wrist. Load suggestions, adding and moving exercises and sets, and notes were added after the first build (see "Phase 8, as built"). With writing to Health on, the watch's HealthKit session (phase 9) keeps the app in front through a rest; with it off, watchOS may put the app away during a long rest, and the rest's notification is what brings the person back.
- **Exercise library source.** Answered: the entries are written in-house. free-exercise-db's license is clear (Unlicense), but only its photos are used, and only by fetching them, so none of its text or data ships in the app.
- **Size.** About 25 sets per session at 4 sessions a week is roughly 5,000 rows a year. That's fine for SQLite, but PR and volume rollups should be memoized and benchmarked in `src/__bench__` like the Today selectors.
- **Retention.** `purgeOldCompletedTasks` can delete the task a session completed. `taskId` is a resolve-or-shrug pointer, and sessions are never purged by the task retention window.
- **Footprint.** The medication area alone appears in 66 files. Expect this area to touch around 70 existing files and add around 50. Most of that is registration the schema, MCP and parity tests demand, so it fails loudly rather than silently, except the reload list, the demo seed, the MCP instructions and the docs, which nothing checks.

## Notes, swapping and updating the routine, as built

Three things on workout mode's exercise card and finish summary. Mocks were reviewed in the PR.

- **Two kinds of note, one new storage rule: none.** A note about the exercise itself ("Every time" on the card) is `Exercise.notes`, saved as an override for a built-in, the same row the exercise editor writes. A note about how it went ("Today") is one note per exercise per workout, kept in the first set's `note` because a session has no row for an exercise (`exerciseDayNote.ts`): reading joins every set's note so one written on a set by the watch or an older version still shows, and writing puts the text on the first set and clears the rest. Removing the first set hands the note to the next. There is no per-set note field in workout mode. `lastTimeNotes.ts` reads last time's off `previousSets` and shows them dated, so they stop appearing once a newer session of the exercise exists and nothing has to clear them. The card shows both notes and last time's in one block, and Add note opens both fields together (Today and Every time, each labeled), not a switch between them, because the two notes are not alternatives and a segmented control read as a choice of one.
- **Every set is one row of fields.** There is no expanded row and no stepper: the cells sit on the table's own columns so Previous, the weight and the reps line up with their headings. Tapping a set's number opens a tray under the row with the plate line, Same as last set and Remove set. A value is a draft until its field loses focus (`TextField` only takes a `value` that differs from what it last reported, so writing each keystroke would turn "10." into "10" mid-type), then goes through `parseSetCell`, which clamps, accepts m:ss for a duration, and snaps back on text that isn't a number.
- **Previous is tappable.** The Previous column, and the "Last time" line on the open set, write last time's numbers into the set (`matchPrevious` pairs the nth warm-up with the nth warm-up and the nth working set with the nth working set).
- **A swap moves only the unticked sets** (`swapSessionExercise`, `exerciseSwap.ts`). A ticked set is a record under the original exercise, so it stays there, and the new exercise sits right after it. The moved sets take the new exercise's own last weights, never the original's. A swap is for today: the routine is not touched.
- **The routine is updated only by the person, one change at a time** (`routineUpdate.ts`). The diff is derived from the routine and the done sets when the summary opens and nothing is stored. Nothing starts ticked, each change carries what it needs so accepting one never depends on another, and Save applies the ticked ones in the same tap that saves the workout. What it compares: working weight (only where the target names one; an empty target means "as last time"), rep range (only when every set missed it the same way), set count, swaps (an exercise in the slot of one the workout skipped), additions, removals and the running order. What it never compares: warm-ups, sets never ticked, rest. A load taken from the progression suggestion is the plan working and is not offered back.

## Routine groups and archive, as built

The Routines tab can put routines under headings and put some away.

- **A group is a name.** `WorkoutRoutine.groupName` (nullable text) says which heading a routine is under, and the synced setting `workoutRoutineGroups` (an ordered list of names, `useWorkoutStore.routineGroups`) is what lets a group exist with nothing in it. There is no group row. `routineGroups.ts` unions the two, so a routine that syncs in under a name the list hasn't heard of yet still gets its heading. A routine is in one group at most. A typed name that matches a group in use ignoring case takes that group's spelling (`resolveGroupName`), so "gym" can't start a second heading. Renaming a group rewrites the list and its routines in one transaction, and a name another group already has joins the two; deleting one leaves its routines in no group.
- **Moving a routine is a drag.** The Routines tab is a `ReorderableList` of headings, routines and an empty group's tile. A routine belongs to the nearest heading above it once dropped (`placementsFromList`), and `arrangeRoutines` writes the order and the groups in one transaction, handing slots back out the way `slotUpdates` does so archived routines keep their place. A routine can't be dragged past the Archived heading (`routineDragRange`): archiving is its own action. There is deliberately no "Move to group" button anywhere.
- **Archived is out of the way, not gone.** `WorkoutRoutine.archived` moves a routine under a collapsed Archived heading and out of its group's heading and count (an archived routine does not keep an otherwise empty group's heading alive). It stays startable from there, stays in its program's rotation, keeps its tasks and its Siri entry; a program's routine picker and the task editor's picker leave it out (the task editor still shows the one already linked). Delete is the only thing that removes it.
- **The rows have their own swipe panels (`RoutineSwipeRow`), deliberately not `SwipeableRow`.** `SwipeableRow`'s left swipe is bulk selection, which this list has no use for, and a routine needs two panels on one side: Archive or Restore, and Delete. Nothing commits on the swipe itself, since both change or remove the routine; each panel runs on its tap. Delete asks through `confirmDelete`, as the editor's does. With iPhone Mirroring on, a "•••" on the row opens the same two actions.
- **Where groups are made.** "New group" is in the header's "…" menu and only asks for a name; the empty group shows a dashed "Add new routine" tile that opens the editor with the group already chosen. A heading's own "•••" has Rename group, Add routine and Delete group.
- **Folds are session state.** A heading folded by hand is a view, not data; Archived starts shut.
- **MCP.** `save_routine` takes `group` and `archived`, `list_workouts` returns them on each routine plus `routineGroups`, and `save_routine_group` / `delete_routine_group` make, rename and delete headings.

## A routine's progress, as built

Files: `src/utils/routineProgress.ts`, `RoutineProgressView.tsx`, and the Edit / Progress control at the top of `RoutineEditorSheet.tsx`. Built to a mock approved in the PR, with the exercise's name moved onto a row of its own so a long name is not cut by the figures beside it.

- **A view of one routine, so it lives in the routine's editor.** A saved routine shows an Edit / Progress control; a new one has no workouts and shows no control. The Progress tab reads the routine as saved, not the staged edits, and tapping an exercise opens its own page inside the editor's Modal (a sibling sheet would never appear).
- **Derived on open, never stored.** The routine's workouts are the finished sessions with its `routineId` (`routineSessions`). A live session is not progress yet, and a workout started some other way does not count toward it.
- **Every figure is this routine's own history.** An exercise's row reads only the sets from those workouts, so "gold" means a heavier top set than any earlier workout of this routine, which can differ from the record on the exercise's page. The row says it is the routine's history only through the footnote.
- **Rows are the routine's exercises as it stands now**, in its order. An exercise taken out drops off; one added later starts at its first workout with it.
- **Volume is `liftVolumeKg`**, so only weighted lifts add to it and a bodyweight routine shows no volume tiles or chart. The percent is first workout to latest, and is withheld with fewer than two workouts.
- **It judges nothing.** A lighter latest top set is a plain negative change in the neutral text color; only a gain is green, and gold is reserved for the latest record, as on the exercise chart.

## Dumbbell weights, as built

A weight is one dumbbell's, always. A set stores `weightKg` of a single dumbbell whether the person logs each
dumbbell or the pair, so records, volume, a progression proposal, a CSV row and the MCP server all read one
comparable number, and changing the choice rewrites nothing (`src/utils/dumbbellLoad.ts`).

- **Two facts, one preference.** *How many dumbbells* is a fact about the movement: `Exercise.dumbbells` (1 or 2),
  defaulting to the library's answer (`ONE_DUMBBELL_IDS`: goblet squat, one-arm row and the like are one, every other
  dumbbell exercise is two; a custom one is two). *How the weight is typed* is a preference: Settings › Workouts ›
  Dumbbell weights (`dumbbellEntry`, each or pair) and, per exercise, `Exercise.loadEntry` overriding it. Both ride
  an override row for a built-in, like `barKg`. The library's own count is stored as null so a correction in a later
  build still reaches a person who changed something else.
- **One dumbbell has no pair.** `entryFor` is `each` for a one-dumbbell exercise whatever is set, so the preference can
  never relabel a goblet squat. `dumbbells` and `loadEntry` are kept only on dumbbell equipment.
- **The scale lives at the edge.** A pair total is halved on the way in (`parseSetCell`'s `scale`, via
  `storedKgFromTyped`) and doubled on the way out (`weightScale` → `formatSetLine`, `valueText`, the record and chart
  text in `workoutRecordText.ts`, the routine Progress tab through `routineProgress`'s `scaleOf` (its volume sums each exercise at its own scale), and the proposal's words through `LoadContext.scale`). The column heading says
  "kg each" or "kg pair" where two dumbbells are in play. A proposal's *loads* are never scaled, only its text.
- **The Apple Watch is sent the shown weights and sends them back.** `watchSetRows`, `watchSuggestion` and
  `watchRecentExercises` scale outward (`WatchWorkoutInput.dumbbellEntry`). Inbound, `tickValuesFromWatch` and
  `parseWatchSession` convert to kilograms before the exercise is known, so `planQuietTaps` takes a
  `weightScaleFor(exerciseId)` and puts each weight back to one dumbbell's (`storedKgFromShown`) in `tick` and for
  `addWatchSession`, comparing the Crown's no-change guard in that space. Changing the setting, or an exercise's entry,
  re-sends the snapshot. A weight the watch sent for a snapshot made before the change is read at the new scale, a
  one-tap window nobody can close without versioning the snapshot.
- **Where it is not applied, on purpose.** CSV export and the MCP server stay per dumbbell, and so does CSV import by default. The import review asks "Dumbbell weights in the file" (Each dumbbell or Pair total, starting at the Settings choice) whenever a file has a weight for a two-dumbbell exercise, and Pair total halves those sets in `buildImportPlan` (`pairTotals`, `heldInTwoDumbbells`) before anything is written. A one-dumbbell exercise is never halved, and a Hevy or Strong file carries no marker of its own, so the person has to say. The MCP says so (`dumbbells` on `list_exercises`, and the rule in `instructions.ts`), so Claude halves a pair total before writing.
  A new surface that shows a dumbbell exercise's weight takes a `scale` from `weightScale` rather than reading
  `weightKg` bare.

## Describing a workout, as built

A routine, or a workout somebody did, can be typed in words and read into the structures above. Mock: `workouts-mocks/describe-routine.png`.

- **Where it is.** Inside the thing it fills, so nobody has to say in advance that they will describe. A "Describe it instead" row in the new-routine editor (`RoutineEditorSheet`, shown only while the form is empty, and filling it in place), and the same row in `LogPastWorkoutSheet` (handing a workout to the import review through `onDescribed`). One sheet, `DescribeWorkoutSheet`, opened with a fixed `mode` (`routine` or `log`) and nested inside the editor's own Modal. The Workouts screen has no Describe button of its own. Neither row exists unless `useAiRoute('workoutDraft')` is not `unavailable`.
- **Claude only, on purpose.** `workoutDraft` has no `ON_DEVICE_ENGINE` entry: it needs the exercise library as context plus a structured reply, which is more than Apple's shared ~4k-token window holds. A keyless fallback (a parser for "bench 3x5 @185") is #4059; it was left out because it would be a second parser to keep in step with the model's. The feature is on by default with Haiku, since this is extraction and the person checks the draft before anything is saved. Its settings row and search entry go with the Workouts area (`AiFeatureMeta.workouts`, the third argument of `aiFeaturesFor`).
- **The model answers with names, never ids.** An id it invented would point at nothing or at the wrong exercise. `draftWorkoutFromText` returns a `WorkoutDraft`; `readWorkoutDraft` range-checks it (an out-of-range count becomes empty rather than a believable number) and caps sets at what the editor holds; `matchExerciseName`, the CSV import's own matcher, settles each name in code. Only an exact name or the same words in another order is decided for the person. Anything else waits on the Match exercises step with the closest match preselected, because a near-miss is often a different lift ("Back squat", "Hack squat"). The step is skipped when every name matches.
- **Nothing is invented, nothing stated is dropped.** A weight, rep count, rest or progression the description didn't give stays empty (an exercise with no sets at all gets the same three the editor gives one added by hand). Text that wasn't a lift goes into Notes, and a set cut for length says so there. A weight with no unit is read in the person's own unit (`workoutUnit`), and the model is told which that is.
- **A routine goes to the routine editor, prefilled** (`RoutineEditorSheet`'s `draft` prop), and is measured against an empty routine so Cancel still asks. An exercise the person chose to create is a `new:` id carrying its description (`RoutineDraft.newExercises`) and is made **when the routine is saved**, never before, so backing out leaves the library as it was. A name that was taken in the meantime is that exercise, not a second one.
- **A workout that happened goes through the import review.** `sessionFromDraft` builds an `ImportedSession` (`source: 'text'`) and `WorkoutImportSheet` reviews it in place of a file (`described` prop): the same name matching, the same duplicate check (a workout starting at the same minute is left out), the same single write through `importSessions`. A day with no time is noon, held to now when that is still ahead; a date in the future is refused, as `log_workout` refuses it. The logical day is what the model is told "today" is. **Apple Health is asked, not assumed.** While Log to Health is on, the review shows an "Add to Apple Health" row (on by default, since the person turned workout logging on), and yes sets `PlannedImportSession.healthWritePending` so the pending-write pass writes it, started at once from the sheet. A file's workouts never get the row: they stay out of Health, because the app they came from may have written them already. The row is offered only when that pass would write it (`describedWorkoutHealthOffer`): with no length in the description (start and end one instant) or more than `PENDING_WORKOUT_WRITE_MAX_AGE_DAYS` ago, the sheet says why instead, and for a missing length says how to fix it.
- **A late reply is dropped.** The request carries a ticket, and a reply that lands after the sheet closed or after another request opens nothing.
- **Into the running workout.** Workout mode has "Describe" beside "Add exercise" (same `useAiRoute('workoutDraft')` gate), opening `DescribeWorkoutSheet` with `into` (and `mode` `routine`): the names step is the routine's, and the settled items go to `addRoutineItems` instead of an editor. The sheet is rendered inside workout mode's own Modal. Sets are always laid out as planned, as a routine start lays an item out (stated numbers, else last time's), and ticking is the person's. A "sets I already did" mode was built and dropped: ticking planned sets is one tap each and the switch was one more thing to read. An exercise already in the workout is left out and named, and a superset whose partner was left out is a plain exercise. A `new:` exercise is made on confirm (`withCreatedExercises`, shared with the routine editor's Save).
- **Into a routine that has content.** The routine editor's "Describe it instead" fills an empty new routine. Once the routine has anything in it (or is a saved one), a quieter "Describe" pill beside "Add exercise" opens the same sheet with `into.target: 'routine'`, and the settled items are appended to the editor's list. An exercise already in the routine is left out and named, and a `new:` exercise they name is held in the editor and made on Save with a draft's own.
- **No MCP change.** An agent already does this by calling `save_routine` and `log_workout` with ids from `list_exercises`.

## Routine and program history, as built

A routine and a program keep their earlier versions, to compare and go back to. Files: `src/utils/routineVersions.ts` (every rule below), the version actions in `useWorkoutStore`, `RoutineHistoryView`, `RoutineVersionSheet`, `ProgramVersionSheet`, `MergeRoutineSheet` and `VersionLabelSheet`, the History card in `ProgramEditorSheet`, and the version row on workout mode's finish summary. Built to the mock approved in the session that planned it (History list, version page, finish summary line, merge picker).

- **Stored, unlike progress.** A plan that is overwritten is gone, so this can't be derived from the workouts the way a routine's progress is. `workout_routine_versions` and `workout_program_versions` hold one row per version (a routine's name, notes and items; a program's name, weeks, rotation, scheme and the version each routine in it was on). They are plans, so they sync like routines and are not in `HEALTH_SYNC_TABLES`. `WorkoutRoutine.currentVersionId` and `WorkoutProgram.currentVersionId` say which row the content is, and `WorkoutSession.routineVersionId` says which version a workout was done on, stamped at `startSession`.
- **A version is what was trained on.** A save that changes the plan (items or notes; a rename is not a new plan) amends the current version until a finished workout has been done on it. After that the editor asks "Save as version N?": a new version, keeping the one those workouts were done on, or an update to it. The person chose to be asked every time over a rule deciding for them. Anything that can't ask passes `version: 'auto'`, which starts a new version once the current one has workouts. That covers an agent over MCP (`source: 'agent'`). Archiving, grouping and filing a routine under a program touch no version.
- **The finish summary is the other way in.** Changes ticked there always make a new version (`source: 'workout'`), since the workout was done on the current one, and its line says so ("Saving makes these version 5"). A workout started from an old version is measured against that version, and its summary offers "Make version 2 current again". Taking a change takes the restore with it, since the changes are to version 2.
- **Numbers are positions in time, never stored.** Two devices each adding a version end up with both, numbered in order, rather than two version 5s. A new version is stamped a millisecond after the newest it follows (`versionTime`), so two saves in one tick still order. `at` back-dates one, for the demo seed only.
- **Before versions were kept.** A routine with no rows has one implicit first version, its content as it stands, under an id every device derives (`spawnSeed.routineVersionInitial`). So a workout can be stamped with it before the row exists. The row is written, from the old plan, the first time the routine changes. A workout with no stamp (from before this, or logged after the fact by `addFinishedSession`, the watch or an import) counts toward the version in effect when it started (`sessionVersionId`). That is right for a workout logged for last week.
- **Restoring never rewrites history.** Restore loads the old plan into the editor as unsaved changes, and Save keeps it as the newest version (`source: 'restore'`). Copy makes a routine of its own from a version (`copyRoutineVersion`, named "{routine} (version N)"). Start a workout from it runs `startSession` with `routineVersionId` and lays out that version's sets. It is not offered where the routine editor sits inside the program editor, since workout mode can't appear over a sheet.
- **Merge folds a copy made by hand into one history** (`mergeRoutines`). The other routine's versions and workouts move over, and each workout keeps the version it counted toward, worked out before the move, since under the new routine time alone would count it toward the wrong one. Its place in any rotation and its open tasks move too, a finished task keeps naming the old routine as provenance, and the routine is deleted. It is refused while a workout from it is in progress. The picker asks once in an alert whatever "Confirm before deleting" says, since a routine leaving the list is the smaller part of what it does.
- **It judges nothing**, as progress doesn't. The version page puts each version's workouts, average volume and dates side by side. An added exercise is drawn in the accent and a removed one in the neutral text color, never green and red.
- **A program version is a block.** Its structure is versioned by the same rule and the same prompt; training maxes are not, since they move every cycle. Each version records the routine versions current when it was saved, and History shows a routine changed partway through a block as "v4 to v5", worked out from the routine versions' times. The program's History is a card in its editor. A row opens `ProgramVersionSheet`, the routine version page's shape for a block: how it went beside the current or previous version, what changed in the rotation, length and progression (`diffPrograms`, counting slots so a routine done twice a cycle compares as two), the version each routine was on, a name, and Restore, which stages the structure into the editor as unsaved changes. A workout from before a program's first version counts toward it, as a routine's does.
- **Deleting** a routine or program deletes its versions. A workout keeps naming the version it was done on, as provenance that may dangle.
- **MCP.** `save_routine` amends or starts versions by the `auto` rule. `list_workouts` gives each routine's current version number and each workout its version. `routine_history` and `program_history` read them, and `restore_routine_version`, `copy_routine_version`, `merge_routines` and `restore_program_version` call the store's own actions. A restore over MCP is always a new version. `unused_features` suggests History when two routines' names read as a copy ("Day One" and "Day One v2", `likelyRoutineCopies`).
